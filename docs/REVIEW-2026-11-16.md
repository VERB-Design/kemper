## 2. THE TWO SCRIM DECISIONS

### Hero scrim (about 0.7 top, 0.85 bottom): keep it. Do not switch to a solid header bar.

**Why the number matters here.** The hero is not one picture. It is a 38-second montage that runs from a dark bar interior, through a bright kitchen, a golfer against a white sky and a youth team around a pale wooden table, to dusk aerials.

Measured against the current scrim, white H1 (48px, large-text bar 3:1):
- Worst sampled frame: **3.16:1** (bowling frame).
- Typical frames: 4-8:1.

White 14px nav text on the transparent header (4.5:1 bar):
- Worst sampled frames: about **4.1-4.6:1**.

So the scrim is already at the floor, not above it. The 0.5 top / 0.62 bottom variant I tested (`scrimB-00.jpg`) simulates at **2.35:1 on the H1 and 2.41:1 on the nav**. A middle variant (0.55 / 0.72) simulates at 2.77 and 3.04. Both fail on the brightest clips. This is a floor: if Troy wants it lighter he can override it knowingly, but the consequence is a headline that goes soft over the table scene.

**Does it still feel premium?** Yes. Look at `d-00.jpg`.
- The top 25% carries a dusk-graded veil.
- From 25% to 40% the footage is untouched.
- The weight sits under the headline, where the eye lands anyway.
- It reads as cinematic grading, not as a lid on the footage. The Chambers Bay frame still has colour and depth.
- The mean veil is about 0.35 across the height. Before today it was roughly 0.22.

**Why not the solid bar.**
- It costs about 80px of footage on every clip.
- It loses the transparent-to-solid transition on scroll, which is the best premium tell on the page (the header already goes solid at 0.94 after 40px).
- It does not touch the real exposure, which is the H1 at the bottom. The bottom scrim stays regardless.
- The solid bar only fixes the nav, and the top veil already does that at 4.1-4.6:1, which is thin.

**If Troy wants more air.** Move only the top veil, not the bottom. One option I have not tested is a quieter top gradient (about 0.5) plus a soft `text-shadow` on `.navlink`. Test it against the kitchen and bowling frames before accepting it.

### Chapter scrim (Who we serve): lighten it. This one is over-built.

Text contrast on the settled chapters, sampled from background bands beside the text:

| Text | Chapter 2 (Clubs & Boards) | Chapter 3 (Owners & Investors) |
|---|---|---|
| Body 18px | 10.2-13.4:1 | 9.1:1 |
| Proof 15px | 10.3:1 | 8.1-9.0:1 |
| Peach eyebrow | 9.8:1 | 7.5:1 |

The bar is 4.5:1. The scrim is paying about double what it needs to. The cost is visible in `d-06.jpg`: the clubhouse, the one image that has to sell "continuity", sits nearly black, and chapter 1's dusk sky goes navy-flat. These photos are the proof moments, so they should carry colour.

**Recommendation.** Keep the horizontal layer (0.82 / 0.55 / 0.05). Cut the added vertical layer's bottom value from **0.7 to about 0.4**.
- By my model this lands body text around 6-7:1.
- That figure is modelled, not rendered, so re-measure after the change.
- Mobile uses 0.68 / 0.92 stacked vertically, and the text sits low where that is heaviest. I would take it to about 0.55 / 0.8 at the same time, but I have not tested that.

This one is a default, not a floor. Anything at or above 4.5:1 on the settled frames is acceptable.

## 3. THE SCALE EFFECTS: strip both

**Hover `scale(1.04)` on `.zoom .ph img`.**
- It affects the three range cards and both Extend cards.
- It breaks the house rule, and nothing is lost by removing it. The arrow already slides 8px and the underline already shifts colour.
- Remove the rule and the matching reduced-motion override.

**Serve chapter `scale(1.12 -> 1)`.**
- The README note says these settle at 1. **That is not true.** I measured the transform at the point each chapter is aligned in the viewport:

| Chapter | Transform when aligned |
|---|---|
| 1 | `scale(1.037)` |
| 2 | `scale(1.019)` |
| 3 | `scale(1)` |

- The scale curve peaks at progress 1/3, 2/3 and 1, but the chapters actually align at 0.23, 0.62 and 1.0. The photos are soft exactly where people stop on them.
- It compounds the next finding. `serve-cities.jpg` is only 1000px wide, so at 1440 it is already upscaled 1.44x before the extra 1.037.
- Fix: return `'none'` unconditionally from the `scale` field in `chapters`. If it needs motion, move the frame (a short `translateX` parallax on the container) or crossfade, but do not enlarge the pixels.
- Also delete the dead `imgScale: 'scale(1.06)'` in `stages`. It is not used in the markup, but it will confuse the next developer.
- Correct the notes entry.

### Major

**M1. House rule: image scale**
- Observation: Section 3. The chapter effect leaves chapters 1 and 2 at 1.037 and 1.019 at rest. The notes claim 1.
- Remediation: Strip both effects as set out in section 3.
- Effort: S (10 min)

**M2. Image resolution (soft at 1x, worse at 2x)**
- Observation: Cover scale is inferred from pixel dimensions against displayed box size, not eyeballed at 2x.

| Asset | Source | Displayed | Cover scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| `serve-cities.jpg` | 1000x667 | 1440x836 chapter | 1.44x |

- Henderson inset is known and not repeated.
- Remediation: Source at least 1920px wide for `serve-cities` and 1600px or more for the `do-*` frames. Repair or replace `do-open`.
- Effort: M (asset dependency)

**M3. Type hierarchy inverts**
- Observation: At 1440 the hero H1 is 48px, capped. The "How we operate" H2 is 49px (`clamp(30px, 3.4vw, 50px)`). There are six different H2 clamps on the page, ranging from 28 to 49px. The page's one H1 is not its loudest headline. Against Aesop and Linear, a 48px H1 in a 990px hero also reads timid rather than quiet.
- Remediation: Set the H1 to about `clamp(36px, 4.8vw, 68px)`. Pull the H2s onto one scale of two sizes (section, sub-section). Keep the stat numerals as the deliberate exception.
- Effort: S

**M4. First screen never says what KemperSports does**
- Observation: The H1 "Entrusted with the places people love." is a good line, but it is the only copy above the fold.
  - On desktop the nav carries Golf, Sports Venues and Live Events.
  - On mobile (`m-00.jpg`) it is H1 plus two buttons, and the hamburger hides every descriptor.
  - A board member or city official first meets "golf courses, private clubs, resorts and sports venues" about 250px below the fold on desktop and a full screen down on mobile.
  - This is a real-world-language failure for a B2B audience that will give it one screen.
- Remediation: Add one supporting line under the H1, in house voice, for example "Management for golf, sports venues and live events." Troy owns the exact words.
- Effort: S

**M5. Mobile: two sections lose their best content**
- Observation:
  - (a) The properties carousel keeps its `16/8` aspect at 390, so each property is a 305x153 sliver (`m-16.jpg`) with two ghosted neighbours. This is the page's showcase moment.
  - (b) "What we do" keeps a 220vh pin on mobile but hides the stage photography (`hide-sm`), so three images never appear and the user scrolls a stuck panel for text that could simply sit open. The notes already unpin it below 680px height, which shows the intended answer.
- Remediation:
  - (a) Use `4 / 3` or `1 / 1` under 760px.
  - (b) Unpin it on mobile, open all three stages, and show each stage's image above its text.
- Effort: M

**M6. Left edges do not agree**
- Observation: At 1440 the page uses five different left edges:
  - 100px: the main content column, which is the hero text, proof, chapters, operate, properties, close and footer.
  - 72px: the serve intro.
  - 48px: the range grid.
  - 45px: the header logo and the Extend block.
  - 36px: the "What we do" block.
- "Start with the challenge in front of you." (`d-10.jpg`) sits visibly left of every other heading, and the logo does not align with the hero text.
- Remediation: Put one gutter token on the whole page. If the range grid and the carousel are meant as deliberate breakouts, make them break out on both sides by the same amount.
- Effort: S-M

**M7. Review panel is on by default**
- Observation: The `tweaksPanel` default is `true`. The Tweaks pill sits over the hero edge, the footer "Privacy Policy" link (`d-16.jpg`) and the footnote line at 390. If that link goes to a client, they see an internal tool.
- Remediation: Default it to `false` for any shared build, or strip TWEAKS:START/END.
- Effort: S

## 9. PRE-MORTEM: what embarrasses us in front of the client

**1. The imagery lets the client down before the copy does.**
- Sources: soft chapter-1 and "What we do" frames, the sky seam, the Henderson inset (M2).
- The hero montage also contains identifiable children (the youth baseball team, the bowling kid) and named-looking staff. Licensing is already noted as unconfirmed. Releases for minors are a different order of problem from stock licensing.
- Mitigation: assets before the review; get releases or swap the clips; keep `hero.mp4` licensing as an open warning.

**2. A board member opens it on a phone between meetings.**
- They get a headline that does not say what the company does (M4), a showcase carousel the size of a postage stamp (M5a), a pinned list with no pictures (M5b) and a 9 MB autoplaying video on cellular.
- Mitigation: M4 and M5; ship a mobile encode (about 1-2 MB) or a poster-only fallback on `prefers-reduced-data` and narrow viewports.

**3. The numbers and the names are read as fact.**
- Beyond the placeholder list already in the notes:
  - The comparative claim "More Top 100 U.S. public and resort courses than any other management company" needs substantiation, not just a source line.
  - The named clients (City of Palm Desert, The Mosaic Company, Village Club of Sands Point) need written permission to be named.
  - `$3B+`, `215+`, `40+ states`, `since 1978`, "11 of 25" and "25+ year partnership" are written, not sourced.
  - The footnotes cite "KemperSports client reporting" and "portfolio data" with no way for a reader to check them.
- Mitigation: add all of these to Content accuracy in the notes with a client sign-off line; the comparative claim goes to the client's legal review first.

## What this review did not verify

- **Real devices.** Nothing was run on a real iPhone or Android. Headless emulation only.
- **Hero footage.** 9 frames of 38 seconds. Headless seeking returned stale frames, so I used real-time playback. The 3.16:1 and 4.1:1 minimums are over that sample, and a brighter frame may exist.
- **Lighter hero scrims.** The numbers for the lighter variants are simulated by backing the current scrim out of JPEG (q70) screenshots and re-applying the alternative. They are good enough to rank the options, not to certify one.
- **Chapter-scrim estimate.** The 6-7:1 figure is modelled, not rendered.
- **2x display softness.** Inferred from pixel dimensions, not viewed.
- **Not re-tested:** keyboard traversal, the reduced-motion path after today's changes, font-loading flashes, LCP, CLS and page weight beyond file sizes.
- **A person still has to close:** whether the 4/3 mobile carousel and the 48px-to-68px H1 feel right, and whether a quieter hero top veil with a nav text-shadow is acceptable.
